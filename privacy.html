Privacy Policy

Last updated 27 November 2026. Vault 55: Games to Earn Money (“Vault 55”) is run by Antreprenor Independent Dumitru Cebotaru (IDNO 1026023023937), Republic of Moldova, trading as 7labs (“we”). Questions: support@dmitru.com.

What we keep

The email you sign in with, if you made an account (with Google, with Apple, or with a password that Firebase stores scrambled; we never see it). With Apple's Hide My Email, that is the relay address Apple gives us. A guest account has no email. Your country, that you confirmed being 18 or older, your play progress, and a record of every Coin and Ingot. To stop cheating: your phone model, system and app version, an install ID made by the app, the store campaign link you installed from (Android only), your time zone offset, the country your SIM card, mobile network and internet address point to (only the country: we do not keep your IP address), and which ads were shown. When the app hits an error or closes unexpectedly: the error text, the app version, the phone model and system, and the install ID, kept 30 days and then only as totals. Before a first reward: your legal name. With each reward request: the payout email you give, which can differ from the one you sign in with.

What we do not keep

Your year of birth is checked and then discarded. We do not ask for contacts, photos or precise location. The optional daily reminder is set on your phone only; we do not keep when or whether you get one.

Details you choose to give

After you make an account we ask for a nickname, age range, gender, interests and how you found Vault 55. Every answer can be skipped, none changes what you earn, and you can change or clear them under Your details in Profile. Your nickname and the days you play are shown to friends, and your nickname also to your crew, players you challenge and players you ask to be friends. You become friends with someone only when you both say yes (joining a crew, or playing a round on a friend's phone, counts as a yes), and you can remove or block anyone. Anyone who is your friend can report your nickname, and we may remove it. We use the rest to understand who plays and, only if you agree, for the totals below.

Two permissions, off unless you tick them

Emails: news and offers from Vault 55, only if you ask for them; stop any time in Profile or with the link in any email. Totals for partners: your answers are added to those of other players who agreed and shown only as rounded totals for groups of 50 or more, one question at a time, never anything about one person. We keep the date and wording of each yes and no. Switch either off in Profile at any time.

Services we use

Google Firebase (Google LLC) signs you in (with Google, with Apple on an iPhone, with email, or as a guest), sends the emails that verify your address or set a new password, and runs our servers. Your account data is stored on Google Cloud. Countries are looked up from internet addresses with IP Geolocation by DB-IP.

Ads and ad networks

Ads are served through Unity LevelPlay (Unity Technologies), which picks an ad from these networks: Unity Ads (Unity Technologies), Google AdMob (Google LLC), Moloco (Moloco, Inc.) and Liftoff Monetize (Liftoff Mobile, Inc., formerly Vungle). Any network added later is named here before it is used.

An ad network gets what it needs to show an ad: your phone's advertising ID, IP address, rough location from it, your phone model and system, and the ads you see or tap. Personalised ads are off unless you turn them on. On an iPhone, if you turned them on, Apple then asks whether ads may use your advertising ID; your answer is kept in the iPhone's Settings and never changes what you earn. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, a consent form from Google asks you before any ad is shown, and its answer decides; change it any time under Ad privacy choices in Profile. When off, the networks may only use this to show, count and protect ads, not to build a profile of you. Their own policies: Unity, Google, Moloco.

Optional surveys

Vault 55 may offer an optional surveys section from BitLabs (Prodege, LLC) and CPX Research (CPX Research GmbH). It opens only after you agree on a separate screen, and you can stop at any time. These partners receive your Vault 55 player ID (never your name or email) and the answers you give them, which may include your age, gender, income and where you live, to match you with surveys. Their use of your answers is covered by their own privacy policies, shown before your first survey.

We do not sell your data

We do not sell your personal data. With personalised ads on, ad networks may use it for ads targeted at you, which some US state laws call sharing. To opt out, turn off Personalised ads in Profile (or use Ad privacy choices where that is shown); it takes effect at once. You can also ask by email. Reports we give partners contain totals only, from players who agreed, never data about one person.

Why we keep it

To run the games, to stop cheating and abuse, to show ads, to send rewards, and for what you agreed to above.

How long we keep it

While your account is open, with these limits. What we know about a phone: 13 months after it was last used. Records of ads shown and survey answers sent to us: 13 months. The moves you made in a level: 90 days, longer only while we look into possible cheating. Friend requests: 30 days. A guest account that earned nothing is deleted after 30 days away. Details in fraud records: 25 months. Fraud records without details and records of rewards already sent are kept as long as the law requires, without your name.

Your choices

You can see your balance and history in the app, change your details, permissions and ad choice in Profile, and delete your account at any time. To ask for a copy of your data or to correct it, write to us.

Deleting your data

Deleting your account erases your identity details and the details you chose to give. The dated record of your permissions stays under your account ID alone, as proof of what was agreed. Records of rewards already sent are kept as accounting records, without your name.
